Abstract—To address cross -asset attack propagation and 
limited defense capacity in multi -asset networks, this paper 
establishes a complete -information, nonzero -sum, open -loop 
differential game. The network state is described by the 
compromised fraction of each asset to characterize how a 
defender dynamically deploys defensive resources. Continuous -
time strategies are parameterized using piecewise -constant 
controls. By combining fourth -order Runge–Kutta integration, 
sequential least -squares programming, and alternating best 
responses, a dynamic defense action decision algorithm (ABR -
DAD) is developed to calculate isolation and recovery intensities 
for each asset and time interval. A directed multi -asset 
propagation network is constructed to examine the 
spatiotemporal allocation of resources under different defense 
capacities. Dynamic defense, fixed -mean defense, and no defense 
are compared in terms of compromised states and cumulative 
losses under the sam e time-varying attack sequence. Simulations 
show that the computed plans satisfy per -time capacity 
constraints and yield differentiated resource allocation. Dynamic 
defense achieves lower cumulative weighted compromise loss and 
cumulative comprehensive defense loss than both reference 
schemes, demonstrating the effectiveness of the method for 
calculating dynamic defensive actions under finite resources. 
Keywords— network defense decision -making, nonzero -sum 
differential game, alternating best response, attack propagation, 
resource allocation 
I. INTRODUCTION 
A multi-asset network comprises interrelated logical assets, 
including entry services, business processing, identity 
authentication, and core data. Attacks can propagate from entry 
points to downstream assets along directed paths, causing 
compromise across multiple assets and business losses. A 
defender can suppress outgoing propagation through source -
side isolation or reduce local compromise through recovery. 
These actions differ in security effects, business impacts, and 
resource consumption. Under limite d capacity, the defender 
must account for the attacker’s strategy when determining the 
type, intensity, and timing of defensive actions. 
Regarding network propagation control and resource 
allocation, Preciado et al.[1] studied the allocation of protection 
resources to inhibit spreading processes in directed networks. 
Al-Eiadeh and Abdallah [2] allocated defense resources to 
interdependent systems using attack graphs and PageRank. 
Paarporn and Xu [3] examined resource tradeoffs between 
preventive and reactive defenses, while Chen et al. [4] 
investigated the effects of protection and recovery investments 
and node priorities on network robustness. These studies 
provide a basis for characterizing propagation structure, asset 
importance, and defense costs. 
In network attack–defense games, Shao and Li [5] used a 
multistage attack–defense graph to examine resource allocation 
during sequential attacks. Hu et al.[6] optimized moving-target 
defense through a Markov differential game, and Lou et al. [7] 
studied dynamic collaborative defense in crowdsensing 
networks under incomplete information. Huang and Zhu[8] used 
a susceptible–infected–susceptible (SIS) propagation model 
and a differential -game framework to study virus suppression 
and adaptive adjustment of connection weights in complex 
networks. Mi et al. [9] introduced stochastic disturbances into 
network attack –defense state evolution and developed a 
stochastic differential -game model for defense strategy 
selection. Tian et al. [10] studied a nonzero -sum attack–defense 
game for malware propagation in cloud environments. Zhang et 
al. investigated real-time defense in scale-free networks[11] and 
potential differential-game decisions in complex networks [12]. 
Sun et al.[13] incorporated action and timing choices into a time 
differential game. These works provide methodological 
foundations for analyzing strategic interactions alongside 
network-state evolution. 
For post -attack repair, Yang et al. [14] employed a 
differential game to study host repair under advanced persistent 
threats (APTs). Wang et al. [15] examined APT lateral 
movement, repair delays, and resource constraints in time -
varying networks. Qin et al. [16] studied defense outsourcing 
under limited budgets through an impulsive differential game. 
Existing work thus covers propagation control, strategic 
interaction, and dynamic repair. This study further addresses 
the joint spatiotemporal allocation of source -side isolation and 
local recovery in a directed propagation network under attacker 
strategy responses and finite capacity constraints. 
This paper undertakes three tasks: constructing a nonzero -
sum, open -loop differential -game model involving directed 
propagation, source -side isolation, and local recovery; 
designing ABR -DAD using piecewise -constant controls and 
alternating best responses to compute defensive actions by 
asset and interval; and analyzing allocations across defense 
capacities while comparing dynamic, fixed -mean, and no -
defense plans under a common attack sequence in terms of 
compromised states and cumulative losses.

continuous-time state system. At the planning origin, both 
players know the network topology, initial state, model 
parameters, objective functions, and constraints, and separately 
choose open-loop control plans over the horizon.

B. ABR-DAD Defense Action Computation

noncooperative games [17] and related iterative work [22], we 
design Alternating Best Response for Dynamic Defense Action 
Decision (ABR -DAD). Its purpose is to produce a defense 
action plan: holding the attack plan fixed, optimize isolation 
and recovery; then, holding the defense plan fixed, compute the 
attack response; and alternate until the numerical stopping 
conditions are met. The procedure is shown in Algorithm 1.

V. CONCLUSION 
This paper establishes a nonzero -sum, open -loop 
differential-game model that incorporates rational attack 
choices and cross-asset propagation, and proposes ABR -DAD 
for dynamic defense action decision-making. Simulations show 
that the algorithm produces differentiated allocations across 
time intervals and assets under all three defense capacities. 
Under the same attack sequence, dynamic defense reduces 
cumulative comprehensive defense loss relative to both no 


PAGE 6
 
 
defense and fixed -mean defense, and mitigates compromise 
accumulation at entry and key internal assets during most 
intervals. Late crossovers in certain asset states further indicate 
the need to evaluate defense plans using a comprehensive 
objective encompassing time -accumulated compromise and 
action costs. Future work will calibrate propagation rates and 
defense costs using measured security -operation data and 
extend network scale and execution feedback to assess defense-
plan updates under real operational conditions.
