ABSTRACT
This paper investigates the optimal allocation of resources between protection and recovery measures to maximize network robustness when
both are available to the defender. Our study finds that as resources increase, a wise defender will quickly shift their focus from recovery to
protection, then gradually back to recovery. We have elaborately detailed this nonlinear phenomenon. In addition, our results show that as
the cost of defending (including protecting and recovering) critical nodes rises, or the load capacity of network redundancy improves, the
defender will allocate a growing share of resources to recovery. The priority assigned to different node classes, i.e., the order of node protection
or recovery, also shapes overall resource allocation.

In the real world, infrastructure networks often face failures
and attacks, leading to task disruptions and functional losses.
Network robustness, a key measure of a network’s adaptabil-
ity under failures or attacks, has become a research hotspot in
complex networks. Research on improving network robustness
has mainly focused on two key aspects: protection measures for
nodes to resist attacks and recovery measures to repair failed
nodes after attacks. However, in practical scenarios where pro-
tection and recovery measures coexist, defenders face the chal-
lenge of sharing limited resources between these two priorities.
While existing research has advanced in terms of individual
strategies, optimal synergistic resource allocation still requires in-
depth exploration. Therefore, this paper proposes an innovative
resource allocation method specifically designed for a network
with cascading failure dynamics. It aims to help defenders allo-
cate limited resources rationally when adopting both protection
and recovery measures. This paper delves deeply into the laws
behind the data from the perspective of statistical analysis, pro-
viding scientific and efficient resource allocation suggestions for
defenders.
I. INTRODUCTION
The infrastructure of complex systems, such as transport
networks,1 power grids,2 and communication networks,3 plays an
important role in our society. Some researchers have identified
similar statistical characteristics among these networks and have
proposed corresponding network structures.4–6 This has laid the
foundation for a better analysis of network science. Recent progress
in network science, a cross-disciplinary field that explores the qual-
itative and quantitative laws of complex networks, has opened up
new avenues for investigating the robustness of these complex net-
works. With the help of network science, we can better analyze
complex systems in the real world. Examples include the analy-
sis of container shipping network topology and routing strategies,7
the simulation of virus propagation,8 and the analysis of corporate
clusters in logistics parks.9
Networks may encounter operational anomalies due to random
failures or intentional attacks, leading to severe consequences like
power outages,10 transportation disruptions,11 and communication
interruptions.12 To enhance performance and mitigate unforeseen
damage, studying the robustness of these infrastructure networks is

With the recent boom in robustness research, awareness of the
importance of network protection has increased. The question of
how to optimize this protection is of great significance, particularly
for determining how a defender should distribute resources for a
more effective defense strategy. Numerous researchers have inves-
tigated this question.24–30 In the early stages, in order to enhance
the robustness of a network, Motter et al. proposed a method for
protecting a network without considering cost.31 Later scholars con-
sidered how nodes can be protected with particle swarm optimiza-
tion algorithms to achieve better gains when considering costs.24
In addition, a local protection strategy has been proposed that can
significantly improve the robustness of a network during cascading
failures.25
Important networks are generally recovered after network fail-
ures. Therefore, some scholars have studied strategies for more
efficient network recovery.32–35 Shang developed a theoretical frame-
work to compare the effects of random recovery and local recovery
in complex networks, which provides a guide for choosing appro-
priate recovery strategies.32 A new tool called the sequential recov-
ery graph has also been introduced to identify critical nodes and
their recovery sequences for optimal recovery.33 In addition, Sun
and Zeng introduced a local betweenness recovery method, which
performs better than traditional methods.34
To the best of our knowledge, most past research on network
robustness has focused on either protective strategies or recov-
ery strategies. However, in reality, both protection and recovery
measures are indispensable for the defender. Focusing solely on pro-
tection measures may lead to business interruption and significant
losses once the defensive line is breached. Conversely, emphasizing
recovery measures alone may lead to frequent attacks and sub-
stantial recovery costs. For instance, for the U.S. electricity grid,
adopting protection measures such as undergrounding transmission
lines is essential to mitigate outages, and the ability of govern-
ments and agencies to swiftly recover the grid following outages is
equally important.36 There is a research gap that creates a disconnect
between theory and practice and requires further investigation.
It is particularly important to consider how a defender should
balance the allocation of resources between protection and recovery
measures in different situations. In this paper, we adopt the per-
spective of the defender and consider how to allocate their limited
resources for optimal network robustness when both protection and
recovery measures are available. We first study the effect of differ-
ent defense strategies and the cost parameter on the optimal defense
resource allocation ratio and network robustness. Subsequently, we
conduct a detailed analysis of how the optimal allocation ratio varies
with changes in total resources. Our study finds that as defense
resources grow, a wise defender will rapidly redirect their resource
allocation from recovery-oriented efforts to protection-oriented ini-
tiatives before gradually reverting to recovery-oriented allocation.
We explain this phenomenon accordingly. Finally, we perform a
sensitivity analysis on the redundancy parameter and extend the
conclusions to networks with different structures.
The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we provide a
detailed description of our cascading failure model and defense
model. In Sec. III, the results and analysis of the simulations are
presented. Finally, we conclude in Sec. IV.
II. MODELS
In this section, we elaborate on the network structure,
attack/defense deployment, cascading failures, and robustness
index. Specifically, the attack/defense deployment involves attack/
defense resources and attack/defense strategies.
A. Network structure
In this paper, we take the Barabási and Albert (BA) scale-free
network6 and Erd˝os–Rényi random (ER) network37 as representative
examples to study how a defender should coordinate their alloca-
tion strategy to maximize the benefit of the defense. On the one
hand, the degree distribution of nodes in a BA network follows
a power-law distribution, so the importance of different nodes in
BA networks varies significantly. Thus, the BA network is a typi-
cal heterogeneous network.38 On the other hand, the edges of the
ER network are randomly generated, and all nodes have approxi-
mately equal importance, so it is a typical homogeneous network.38
Since many networks are often heterogeneous in reality,39 our main
conclusions focus on BA network analysis. Moreover, given the
existence of homogeneous networks in reality, we also conduct cor-
responding analyses on an ER network to illustrate the universality
of our conclusions.

2. Attack and defense strategy
There exist two principal types of attack and defense strate-
gies: random and deliberate.13,15,41 In a random strategy, the nodes
to be attacked or defended are selected randomly. In contrast, the
deliberate strategy selects nodes based on specific indicators. This
study aims to explore, in realistic load-failure scenarios, how to allo-
cate defense resources between protection and recovery measures
to achieve better network robustness. This section clarifies the sce-
nario considered in this paper, i.e., the specific strategies adopted
by the attacker/defender. In real life, it is much more common for
attackers to have no knowledge of the network architecture and to
only be able to perform random attacks.43 Previous research44 has
extensively examined the influence of strategic interplay between
attackers and defenders on network robustness from the game the-
ory perspective. It is noteworthy that when considering deliberate
attacks and deliberate defenses with equal resources, the attack strat-
egy proves to be completely ineffective.44 This result is intuitive and
easy to understand. Consequently, the practical implications wor-
thy of discussion in this context are quite limited. Therefore, this
paper focuses on the scenario in which the attacker employs random
attacks. Furthermore, to achieve optimal defense outcomes, defend-
ers often adopt deliberate defense rather than random defense. For
example, modern power grid defense requires a purposeful, planned,
and organized approach to addressing various threats.45,46 There-
fore, this paper focuses on the scenario in which the defender adopts
deliberate defense.
This paper focuses on two types of defense strategies: pro-
tection and recovery. Two possible strategies are also considered
for allocating protection resources. One is high degree protection
(HDP). This works by first sorting the nodes by descending degree,
then protecting them sequentially in that order. The other is Low-
Degree Protection (LDP), which works by sorting the nodes by
ascending degree, then protecting the nodes one by one in the sorted
sequence. When τ > 0, HDP can better protect large-degree nodes
so that the network’s topology is not damaged by the large nodes,
while LDP can be used to protect more nodes.
This paper also considers two types of recovery strategies.
To recover important nodes among the failed ones, the defender
employs the High-Degree Recovery (HDR) strategy. Similar to
HDP, HDR first arranges the failed nodes by descending degree,
then recovers them in sequence. Conversely, Low-Degree Recovery
(LDR) first targets nodes with lower degrees. It arranges the failed
nodes according to an ascending degree sequence and recovers them
successively.
Ultimately, there are four different defense strategies: HHS
(HDP and HDR strategy), HLS (HDP and LDR strategy), LHS (LDP
and HDR strategy), and LLS (LDP and LDR strategy).

protection and recovery measures to achieve better network robust-
ness. Therefore, we next explain in detail the previously mentioned
nonlinear trend of θ ∗

ownership status of resources, we divide this nonlinear process into

resources are sufficient (e.g., λ = 90%), the defender allocates all
resources to recovery measures for the highest return. To intuitively

C. Sensitivity analysis of the redundancy parameter α
To gain a more comprehensive understanding of the network
defense resource allocation theory, we analyzed the impact of the
redundancy parameter α on the optimal allocation ratio θ ∗
p and net-
work robustness. Figure 8(a) shows G as a function of the resource
allocation ratio θp for different redundancy parameters α. As shown
in Fig. 8(b), an increase in the redundancy parameter α leads to a
decrease in the optimal allocation ratio θ ∗
p . This implies that as α
increases, a good defender will tend to devote more resources to
recovery measures. This is because an increase in α increases the
load capacity of the nodes and the network’s ability to withstand
cascading failures. The severity of the first round of cascading fail-
ures triggered by the attack is correspondingly reduced. Specifically,
fewer nodes experience failures due to overload, diminishing the sig-
nificance of protection. Moreover, the second round of cascading
failures after recovery is also weakened, which further bolsters the
effectiveness of the recovery measures. In summary, as α increases,
a good defender speeds up the process of tilting resources toward
recovery measures.
Figure 8(c) shows G∗as a function of α. As α increases, G∗
increases, and the increase in G∗becomes smaller and smaller.
This is because as α increases, the extra load capacity of each node
increases, and the network becomes more robust to damage. At the
same time, as α increases, more and more capacity is wasted, which
results in the subsequent increase in the redundancy factor playing
a smaller role in the increase of G.

IV. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we have investigated how a defender should
adjust the proportion of resources allocated to protection and recov-
ery measures to achieve better defense gains. We have found that in
both Barabási and Albert scale-free (BA) networks and Erd˝os–Rényi
random (ER) networks, when resources are scarce (i.e., λ = 0.1%),
a good defender allocates all the resources to recovery measures.
As the total resources increase, a wise defender will quickly shift
their focus from recovery measures to protection measures and then
gradually back to recovery measures. Specifically, as total resources
increase, the optimal allocation ratio θ ∗
p first increases to a peak,
then decreases. We have analyzed this nonlinear process in detail.
In addition, different defense strategies significantly affect how the
defender allocates the ratio. Overall, the recovery strategy has a
greater impact on the optimal allocation ratio. When the recovery
strategy is Low-Degree Recovery (LDR), the trough of G∗will appear
later. Similarly, parameters such as τ and α also affect the optimal
allocation ratio and network robustness. As τ continues to increase,
a wise defender tends to allocate resources to recovery measures.
Furthermore, as τ increases, the optimal network robustness that
can ultimately be achieved typically shows a downward trend. As
the redundancy factor α increases, a good defender will be more
inclined to allocate more resources to recovery measures. We also
analyzed the corresponding results in an ER network. When α is
small, G∗of the ER network is extremely small. Compared with the
optimal allocation ratio in BA networks, that in ER networks is less
affected by changes in the cost parameters τ and defense strategies.
This work can provide insights to help defenders better allocate
resources from a statistical analysis perspective. In addition, there is
still some work worth further research, such as developing a game
model of defenders with protection and recovery measures vs attack-
ers. This involves simulating how attackers dynamically adjust their
attack strategies in response to the allocation of defense resources.
This helps defenders better allocate resources when facing complex,
evolving attacks in the real world.
