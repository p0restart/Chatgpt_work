Collaborative defense model 
for crowdsensing networks based 
on incomplete Information differential games
Kaiwen Lou1, Guosheng Zhao1*   , Jian Wang2, Xiaodan Huang1 and Zixuan Wan1 
Abstract 
Crowdsensing networks face complex security threats due to their open architecture and distributed collaboration 
model. However, existing defense methods neglect the heterogeneity of sensing nodes and information asymmetry 
between attackers and defenders, resulting in traditional static defense strategies that struggle to adapt to dynamic 
adversarial environments. To address this challenge, this paper proposes a dynamic collaborative defense model 
driven by incomplete information differential games. Firstly, a dynamic differential equation system with random 
perturbation factors is established to characterize the security state evolution process of sensing nodes. Subse-
quently, an incomplete information differential game model is constructed to analyze attack and defense behaviors, 
with a Bayesian strategy inference method designed to infer potential strategy preferences of attackers through real-
time attack-defense interaction results and node states. This approach overcomes the limitations of static belief 
updates in traditional incomplete information games. Finally, a defense decision-making algorithm is designed based 
on optimal control theory. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed model accurately characterizes strategic 
interactions between attackers and defenders while providing real-time defensive strategies based on the inference 
method, improving defense success rates by 24.2%. Additionally, the model exhibits strong adaptability in dynamic 
network environments, enhancing network availability by 22.3%.
Keywords  Incomplete information differential games, Collaborative defense, Crowdsensing networks, Bayesian 
inference, Attack and defense strategy selection
Introduction
With the rapid development and widespread application 
of information and communication technologies, crowd-
sensing networks, as an emerging perception paradigm, 
have become integral components of modern network 
systems (He et al. 2022). Characterized by widely distrib-
uted nodes, dynamic adaptability, and reliance on shared 
information, this network paradigm plays crucial roles in 
various domains, including smart transportation (Li 
et al. 2023), environmental monitoring (Shi et al. 2025), 
and smart agriculture (Sun et al 2022), thereby facilitat-
ing the advancement of the Internet of Things and intel-
ligent systems. Mobile crowdsensing networks (MCNs) 
leverage diverse sensing nodes that collectively par-
ticipate in data acquisition and transmission, forming 
complex collaborative network ecosystems. However, 
the growing interactions with external systems expose 
crowdsensing networks to sophisticated security threats 
and attack challenges, potentially resulting in severe 
consequences such as compromised network function-
ality, degraded quality of service, and complete system 
failure. Consequently, developing effective collaborative

defense mechanisms to secure crowdsensing networks 
has emerged as a critical research challenge.
Attacks against crowdsensing networks are charac-
terized by their diversity, complexity, and persistence. 
Attackers typically target vulnerable nodes in the net-
work through various attack vectors, including data theft, 
denial of service attacks, node occupation, data tam-
pering, identity disguise, and malicious code injection 
(Xiong et al. 2021; Zhao et al. 2025; Liang et al. 2020). In 
response to these security threats, the academic commu-
nity has conducted extensive research on attack defense 
in crowdsensing networks. Simsek et  al. (Simsek et  al. 
2020) employed sequence feature selection and knowl-
edge-based methods to enhance deep neural network 
performance in detecting false tasks within crowdsens-
ing networks. Huang et al. (Huang et al. 2024) designed a 
novel consensus protocol called Proof-of-Data (PoD) and 
a homomorphic data perturbation scheme to construct 
a decentralized mobile crowdsensing platform ensuring 
system security, data privacy protection, and incentive 
fairness. Zhao et al. (Zhao et al. 2023) proposed a crowd-
intelligence collaborative defense model based on a 
multi-objective discrete Harris Hawk optimization algo-
rithm to address DDoS attacks in MCNs by establishing 
a distributed collaborative mechanism with overlapping 
perception alliances and a Pareto-optimal defense solu-
tion screening strategy. However, existing network secu-
rity defenses primarily focus on single-point defense 
strategies targeting specific attack types, potentially 
resulting in fragmented and inconsistent defense mecha-
nisms that fail to address persistent attacks in dynamic 
environments. Consequently, there is an urgent need to 
develop collaborative decision-making frameworks capa-
ble of coordinating heterogeneous node capabilities and 
dynamically optimizing defense strategy allocation to 
counter complex persistent attack threats in crowdsens-
ing networks.
Game theory, as a structured theoretical framework for 
analyzing strategic interactions, provides a powerful tool 
for addressing cybersecurity defense decision-making 
problems (Yao et  al. 2024). By modeling the behavioral 
characteristics and patterns of attackers throughout the 
cyber-attack lifecycle, game-theoretic approaches can 
facilitate the development of optimal mitigation strate-
gies that minimize attack-induced damage while consid-
ering associated costs and achieving balanced trade-offs. 
In this context, differential games, as mathematical tools 
for describing continuous-time decision-making pro-
cesses, are particularly well-suited for modeling strategy 
evolution processes in dynamic cybersecurity scenarios. 
Zhang et al. (Zhang et al. 2018) first applied differential 
games to network security defense decisions, while sub-
sequent studies (Zhang et al. 2023a; Zhang et al. 2023b; 
Wu et al. 2022) have constructed differential game mod-
els for various network attack and defense scenarios and 
designed corresponding defense strategy selection algo-
rithms. However, most existing differential game studies 
assume that attackers and defenders possess complete 
information—that is, both parties have a comprehensive 
understanding of each other’s payoff functions, strategy 
sets, and system states. This assumption is inconsistent 
with real-world network environments where attackers’ 
behaviors are concealed and defenders cannot fully com-
prehend attackers’ intentions and strategic information.
Aiming at the above problems, this paper proposes 
an incomplete information differential game model 
that integrates Bayesian inference with differential 
game theory to achieve collaborative optimization of 
attack behavior prediction and defense decision-making 
through dynamic interaction mechanisms. In this model, 
defenders infer potential strategy distributions of attack-
ers in real-time based on Bayesian inference and employ 
dynamic differential equations from differential game 
theory to characterize the real-time evolution of sens-
ing node states. Through this mechanism, defenders 
can gradually reduce uncertainty about attackers’ strate-
gies under information asymmetry conditions, thereby 
transforming traditional passive response approaches 
into proactive defense methods. This approach not only 
dynamically adapts to complex multi-stage attack sce-
narios but also establishes a real-time and continuously 
adaptive defense decision system for crowdsensing 
networks. The main contributions of this paper are as 
follows:
(1)	 A differential game framework for crowdsens-
ing networks with incomplete information is con-
structed. By establishing a dynamic differential 
equation system with a random interference inten-
sity factor, the state evolution process of sensing 
nodes in complex attack and defense environments 
is accurately portrayed. The limitations of exist-
ing models in real networks are overcome, and the 
adaptability of the models to real network environ-
ments is significantly improved.
(2)	 A dynamic Bayesian strategy inference method 
is designed to realize real-time strategy inference 
based on continuous interaction information. The 
method dynamically infers the attacker’s poten-
tial attack strategies using real-time network state 
and attack-defense utility difference and enables 
the defender to capture the subtle evolution of the 
attacker’s strategy.
(3)	 The Hamiltonian optimal control method is used to 
solve for maximizing attack and defense gains, and 
equilibrium existence is analyzed and proved. An

uncertain network environment is developed. The

Related work
In recent years, the research and application of game 
theory in crowdsensing networks have become increas-
ingly in-depth. Studies have shown that it can charac-
terize the dynamic game relationship between network 
participants (such as service providers, requesters, and 
malicious attackers) and provide theoretical support for 
key issues in crowdsensing networks, including sensing 
cost, data quality, service pricing equilibrium decision, 
and node incentives (Dasari et al. 2020). Yang et al. (Yang 
et  al. 2017) proposed a mobile crowd intelligence data 
assurance method that integrates unsupervised quality 
evaluation and Shapley value-based cooperative game 
incentives. It improves data reliability by filtering low-
quality data through anomaly detection and designing 
a game-driven surplus allocation strategy. Cheung et al. 
(Cheung et al. 2018) constructed a two-stage Stackelberg 
game framework between service providers and users, 
combined with dynamic programming algorithms and 
closed-form decision criteria, and achieved a coordinated 
equilibrium of service provider profit maximization and 
user participation strategy optimization under network 
heterogeneity constraints. Jin et al. (Jin et al. 2017) pro-
posed the Theseus payment mechanism. Constructing 
a Bayesian Nash equilibrium under a non-cooperative 
game, it encourages workers in MCS to maximize their 
perception efforts to improve data quality, thereby opti-
mizing the aggregation accuracy of the truth discov-
ery algorithm while ensuring individual rationality and 
budget feasibility. However, the above studies mainly 
focus on aspects such as data quality, policy scheduling 
and incentive mechanisms in crowdsensing networks, 
and pay less attention to the security and defense issues 
of crowdsensing networks.
In reality, network attack and defense are character-
ized by real-time, continuous processes, and the attack 
and defense strategies are dynamic and change at high 
frequency. Therefore, only continuous, real-time defense 
strategies are applicable to actual attack-defense inter-
actions and can improve the defense effect. Differential 
games provide an effective framework for describing 
the dynamic evolution of network security status and 
the real-time decision-making process in attack-defense 
interactions. Gan et  al. (Gan et  al. 2024) proposed a 
device classification-driven differential game collabo-
rative defense framework for APT attack and defense 
scenarios in the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT): 
hierarchical modeling based on differences in device 
protection capabilities, a differential game model for het-
erogeneous node attack and defense cost–benefit opti-
mization, and a dynamic defense plan generated through 
a saddle point equilibrium strategy. Rao et al. (Rao et al. 
2020) proposed an epidemiological defense model based 
on the quarantine mechanism for DDoS attack protec-
tion of critical infrastructure networks. The local and 
global stability conditions of the infection-free/endemic 
equilibrium point were analyzed by constructing a differ-
ential equation system, and its effectiveness was verified 
through numerical simulation. Wang et  al. (Shengling 
Wang et  al. 2021) proposed a differential game-driven 
attack and defense dynamic analysis framework, con-
structed a black-box and white-box dual model to 
describe the evolution law of network confrontation, and 
solved the equilibrium solution through the Nash equi-
librium existence proof and iterative algorithm, providing 
theoretical support for the design of network attack and 
defense dynamic strategies. Zhu et al. (Zhu et al. 2023) 
proposed a dynamic attack and defense game frame-
work driven by multi-agent deep reinforcement learning 
(MADRL), constructed a multi-defender-multi-attacker 
collaborative confrontation model, broke through the 
traditional single attack and defense subject assump-
tion, and realized the real-time generation and optimi-
zation of defense strategies through equilibrium analysis 
and experience sharing mechanism. Zhang et al. (Zhang 
et  al. 2020) constructed a multi-stage real-time attack 
and defense game model and designed a total discounted 
benefit equilibrium strategy selection algorithm by inte-
grating differential games and Markov decisions. Table 1 
analyzes and summarizes the main research on differen-
tial games in network security defense.
Although differential games can more accurately 
describe the attack and defense process in continuous 
time, most existing studies assume that the attacker and 
defender have complete information, that is, both par-
ties have a complete understanding of each other’s payoff 
functions, strategy sets, and system states. However, in

actual networks, the attacker’s behavior is often hidden, 
and the defender cannot fully grasp his intentions and 
strategy information. This information asymmetry limits 
the applicability of differential game models.
Incomplete information games provide a theoreti-
cal framework for solving the information asymmetry 
problem. In an incomplete information game, partici-
pants estimate the opponent’s strategy through a priori 
beliefs and continuously update the beliefs through the 
feedback data during the game. Patra et al. (Patra et al. 
2024) proposed a Bayesian reasoning-driven repeated 
game evolution strategy. By dynamically accumulating 
evidence of opponent behavior and updating strategy 
beliefs, they revealed the evolutionary stability condi-
tions of Bayesian learning strategies against exploi-
tation in repeated prisoner’s dilemmas. Zhang et  al. 
(Liu et  al. 2021) proposed a defense decisions model 
that integrates partially observable Markov deci-
sion and cyclic deep Q networks. It breaks through 
the traditional MDP complete rationality assumption 
and dynamically approximates the game equilibrium 
strategy under incomplete information conditions 
through online learning. Fang et al. (Fang and Yi 2024) 
proposed a Bayesian learning-driven adaptive path 
planning strategy. For the limited rational attack and 
defense game scenario, they dynamically inferred the 
attacker’s reasoning level by integrating the level-
k decision model and the multi-model strategy. The 
defensive adaptability under unknown attack strate-
gies was experimentally verified. Ren et al. (Ren et al. 
2023) proposed a multi-UAV cooperative air combat 
decision-making method driven by incomplete infor-
mation dynamic games. They inferred the enemy’s tac-
tical intentions through a dynamic Bayesian network 
and solved the Bayesian Nash equilibrium strategy 
based on a multi-agent deep reinforcement learning 
framework. The simulation verified the effectiveness of 
the cooperative tactical generation and execution in a 
complex confrontation environment. Hu et al. (Hu et al. 
2018) proposed a bounded rational Bayesian attack 
and defense evolutionary game model. By introducing 
a selection intensity factor to improve the replication 
dynamic equation, expanding the multi-type strategy 
set, and integrating incomplete information scenarios, 
they constructed the evolution trajectory of the defense 
strategy under the dynamic confrontation network and 
realized the generation of the optimal defense algo-
rithm based on evolutionary stable equilibrium.
Among the above works, the current work lacks 
research on the incomplete information differen-
tial game model. This paper proposes a collaborative 
defense method for crowdsensing networks based 
on incomplete information differential games. This 
method overcomes the information asymmetry prob-
lem by integrating the dynamic modeling capabilities 
of differential games with the Bayesian strategy infer-
ence method of incomplete information games and 
introduces a collaborative mechanism to optimize the 
overall strategy selection of multiple defenders, thereby 
providing a new solution for the security protection of 
complex dynamic networks.

Incomplete information differential game model
This section is dedicated to introducing the evolutionary 
dynamics model of attack and defense in crowdsensing 
networks. First, we describe the reality of crowdsensing 
networks under attack. Then, we abstract this description 
into a mathematical framework for better analysis.
Model overview and game theory model definition
In a crowdsensing network, attackers will launch vari-
ous attacks against sensing nodes. In particular, attacks 
against key nodes may cause damage or paralysis of the 
entire network. Defenders need to monitor the network 
status in real-time, identify possible attack behaviors, and 
deploy corresponding defense measures. It is worth not-
ing that there is information asymmetry between attack-
ers and defenders, and neither party can fully understand 
the other’s strategy and capabilities. The attack and 
defense process in a crowdsensing network is essentially 
a dynamic competitive game process for the control of 
sensing nodes under incomplete information conditions. 
This complex interaction can be mathematically modeled 
and analyzed through the incomplete information differ-
ential game theory, thus providing a theoretical basis for 
network collaborative defense.
Figure 1 illustrates the overall framework of the pro-
posed incomplete information differential game col-
laborative defense model. This framework consists of 
three core steps: First, a state-aware module monitors 
the state of sensing nodes and the results of attack-
defense interactions in real time; second, Bayesian pol-
icy inference dynamically updates the belief estimate 
of the opponent’s policy, and solves for the equilibrium 
policies of both sides based on optimal control theory; 
finally, the attacking and defending sides execute their 
policies to form an adversarial process, the results of 
which drive the evolution of node states according to 
differential equations, thus constituting a closed-loop 
feedback control mechanism.
There are two players in the incomplete informa-
tion differential game, an attacker and a defender. The 
attacker’s goal is to maximize the system loss by assign-
ing appropriate attack competition intensity to each 
sensing node to paralyze the sensing node. The defend-
er’s goal is to protect each sensing node from attack by 
assigning appropriate defense competition intensity to 
each sensing node, thereby minimizing the system loss.

Network model and sensing node status
Combined with the CICIoMT2024 dataset (Dadkhah 
et  al. 2024) scenario, this dataset constructs a medical 
Internet of Things (IoMT) containing 40 devices. These 
devices are connected via Wi-Fi, MQTT, and Bluetooth, 
and continuously collect and transmit patient physiologi-
cal data. In this environment, attackers launch various 
attacks such as DDoS, DoS, and reconnaissance against 
these IoMT devices. Therefore, an example of the attack 
and defense model is shown in Fig.  2. The Q sensing 
nodes are considered to be node resources that the two 
parties in the game compete for. The goal of the attacker 
and the defender is to gain control of the sensing nodes 
by assigning appropriate competition intensity. ED(t) and 
EA(t) are defined as the competition intensity assigned 
by the defender and the attacker to the crowdsensing 
networks sensing nodes at time t, respectively. The game 
participants with higher competition intensity can con-
trol the sensing nodes. Each sensing node is categorized 
into four states, which are normal state ( N ), impaired 
state ( I ), secure state ( S ), and crisis state ( C ). The total 
duration of crowdsensing network attacks is represented 
by T . At the moment t , the number of sensing nodes in 
N , I , S , and C states are xN(t) , xI(t) , xS(t) , xC(t),where 
∀t ∈[t0, T] , xN(t) , xI(t) , xS(t) , xC(t) ≥0 . And it satisfies 
Eq. (1).
(1)	 N is a normal state: it means that the sensing 
nodes function normally and can provide services 
to the network. These nodes are controlled by the 
defender by default. However, these nodes have 
inherent security vulnerabilities, and attackers can 
compete for control by assigning higher competi-
tion intensity.
(2)	 I is an impaired state: it means that the sensing 
node has been controlled by the attacker, but it 
can still provide normal services to the network on 
the surface, making it difficult for the defender to 
immediately detect its controlled state. The attacker 
can utilize this node to attack neighboring nodes 
or collect sensitive information for subsequent 
attacks. The node in this state requires the defender 
to invest more competition intensity to restore con-
trol.
(3)	 S is a secure state: it means that the sensing node 
has been effectively protected by the defender 
through higher competition intensity. A node in 
a secure state has high immunity to the attacker’s 
attempts and cannot be breached by the attacker.
(4)	 C is a crisis state: is a crisis state: it means that the 
sensing node has been completely controlled by 
the attacker, the service quality has been severely 
degraded or has been maliciously tampered with, 
and it can no longer provide reliable services to the 
network. Meanwhile, the defender’s defense meas-
ures are ineffective against it.

and defense response capabilities among different nodes.

of the network state by both attackers and defenders. To 
characterize the aggregation effect of node heterogene-

defender’s defensive effectiveness when protecting heter-

on the overall attack and defense posture while ensuring 
computational efficiency. These perturbation terms fol-

Bayesian inference
In actual network attack and defense confrontation, the 
attacker and defender can-not fully observe the strategy 
adopted by the opponent, which constitutes an incom-
plete information game environment. In order to solve 
the problem of incomplete information, this study 
adopts Bayesian inference to estimate the opponent’s 
poten-tial strategy choices. Game participants observe 
the state changes of sensing nodes in the network and 
the attack-defense utility difference as known infor-
mation to infer the strategy that the other party may 
adopt in the next round of the game, thereby provid-
ing a decision-making basis for choosing the optimal 
counter-strategy. Figure 4 shows how game participants

The computational complexity of the above algo-
rithm mainly consists of two parts: Bayesian inference 
and iterative solution of Hamiltonian equations. For 
a network containing Q sensing nodes, step 7 of the 
algorithm performs Bayesian inference, with a time 
complexity of O(m + n), where m and n are the num-
ber of defense and attack strategies, respectively. Steps 
8–13 of the algorithm perform common-state func-
tion calculation and strategy optimization. According 
to Eqs.  (31, 32 and 35), the common-state functions 
of the four state variables and the optimal solutions of 
m defense strategies are calculated, with a complexity 
of O(m). The entire iterative loop is executed T 
times, so the total time complexity of the algorithm 
is O(T·(m + n)). Compared with the defense methods 
based on deep neural networks, which usually require 
a training cost of O(Q2·E) (E is the number of training 
rounds), this algorithm avoids the computational over-
head of forward and backward propagation of neural 
networks, and the complexity is decoupled from the 
number of nodes Q , and is only affected by the size of 
the policy space and the number of time slices. In terms 
of space complexity, the algorithm mainly stores the 
node state vectors and policy belief distributions, with 
a memory usage of O(Q + m + n), which is far lower 

Page 16 of 25
Lou et al. Cybersecurity           (2026) 9:143 
than the parameter storage requirements of deep learn-
ing models.
From the perspective of practical deployment, this 
algorithm has the potential to run in real time in mobile 
crowd sensing scenarios. First, the Bayesian poste-
rior probability update (13)–(14) of the algorithm only 
involves probability multiplication and normalization 
operations. The optimal strategy solution formulas (32)–
(35) are in analytical form and can be directly substituted 
into the calculation without iterative search, resulting 
in low computational cost per decision. Second, com-
pared with defense methods based on deep neural net-
works, this algorithm does not require an offline training 
stage and can directly perform online reasoning and 
decision-making based on the real-time observed differ-
ence in attack and defense utility ω(t) and node state x(t) , 
making it suitable for dynamically changing crowd sens-
ing environments.

proposed method in real-world IoT attack environments.
It should be noted that the CICIoT2023 dataset con-
tains only 105 IoT devices, and its network size is not 
large enough to fully represent a large-scale swarm intel-
ligence sensing network. However, the attack and defense 
risk rates VA and VD (Eqs. 10–11) are driven by the ratio 
of the control nodes of both sides, so that the state transi-
tion rate in the state evolution equation Eq. (12) is adap-
tively adjusted with the network size. The qualitative 
characteristics of the game behavior do not depend on 
the absolute size of the network. Therefore, in the simu-
lation, the behavior of networks of different sizes can be 
simulated by adjusting the total number of nodes Q . The 
experimental results in Sect.  “Model validity analysis” 
( Q =1000) and this section ( Q =105) both show consist-
ent phased convergence characteristics, which verifies 
this property. In addition, the time complexity of the 
algorithm is O(T·(m + n)), which is decoupled from the 
number of nodes Q and will not significantly increase the 
computational overhead due to the increase in network 
size.
Conclusion
In response to the security challenge of information 
asymmetry between attackers and defenders in crowd-
sensing networks, this paper proposes a collaborative 
defense decision-making framework based on incom-
plete information differential games. To adapt to the 
characteristics of incomplete information between 
attackers and de-fenders, a dynamic defense mechanism 
integrating Bayesian inference is constructed, enabling 
defenders to make optimal decisions under informa-
tion uncertainty. On this basis, the evolution process 
of crowdsensing network security state is modeled as a 
differential equation system, and the model’s adaptabil-
ity to environmental uncer-tainty is enhanced by intro-
ducing random perturbation parameters. At the same 
time, a saddle point equilibrium solution method based 
on Bayesian strategy inference is designed to ensure that 
the defender can generate the optimal defense strategy 
based on limited observation information. Simulation

experimental results show that the proposed method 
can effectively maintain network security under different 
attack scenarios and random perturbation conditions. 
Compared to traditional defense schemes, the approach 
has obvious advantages in network availability and can 
achieve strategy-adaptive adjustment when key param-
eters, such as node recovery rates change. Algorithm 
complexity analysis shows that the proposed method has 
a time complexity of O(T·(m + n)) and a space complex-
ity of O(Q + m + n), and each step of the algorithm can be 
executed independently to support distributed deploy-
ment. Simulation experiments at different scales demon-
strate that the model’s policy evolution mechanism has 
cross-scale consistency, preliminarily verifying its feasi-
bility for scaling to larger-scale networks. This research 
offers a new technical approach for crowdsensing net-
works security protection, breaking through the limita-
tions of traditional static defense strategies and providing 
theoretical support for collaborative defense decisions in 
practical applications.
Future work will be carried out in the following aspects: 
(1) Further improve the random perturbation model and 
study the evolution law of defense strategies in more 
complex and uncertain environments; (2) Although this 
paper has verified the computational efficiency of the 
model in the experimental environment, the Bayesian 
inference and differential equation solving mechanism 
of the model still face the challenge of increased com-
putational overhead when deployed in ultra-large-scale 
networks. In the future, we will explore the lightweight 
design of the model by combining deep reinforcement 
learning and other methods to further improve the mod-
el’s adaptability to large-scale heterogeneous networks; 
(3) Study the collaborative decision-making mechanism 
of multiple defenders and build a more efficient distrib-
uted defense framework; (4) Expand the model to deal 
with more types of complex attack scenarios and improve 
the versatility and robustness of the defense strategy;(5) 
Conduct deployment verification of the algorithm in 
real-world mobile crowd sensing scenarios and evaluate 
its real-time decision-making performance on typical 
edge computing platforms; (6) The current dataset vali-
dation experiments are limited by the network size of the 
public dataset. In the future, the scalability and applica-
bility of the model will be further validated on a larger 
real dataset.
