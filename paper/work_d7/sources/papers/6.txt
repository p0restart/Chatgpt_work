Most defense strategies in complex networks are developed from the defense perspective, overlooking 
the key attack-defense characteristics in cybersecurity. A defense decision algorithm is ineffective when 
dealing with dynamic attacking behaviors, and when based on attack-defense analysis, stochastic uni- 
form network models are generally used to model the target network, while most networks are large 
and complex. Thus, the algorithms and their results do not well suit small-world, scale-free, and high- 
aggregation networks. In this study, considering the structural characteristics of complex networks and 
the attack-defense characteristics of cybersecurity, potential differential game theory is integrated with 
complex networks, and a global optimal defense decision algorithm is proposed according to the over- 
all network defense objective. Based on the evolutionary analysis of network security states, a network 
attack-defense potential differential game model is constructed. Adversarial analysis is carried out on the 
overall attack-defense strategy, and a defense decision algorithm is designed based on a saddle point 
equilibrium strategy. Simulation tests are carried out on small-world and scale-free networks to evaluate 
the effectiveness of the proposed method by comparing its performance with that of random defense 
strategies and classic decision algorithms.

1. Introduction 
With the development of information technology, numerous cy- 
bersecurity issues have been reported ( Vaz, 2019 ; Payne and Mie- 
nie, 2021 ). A safe network is essential, and its defense against cy- 
berattacks is a top priority. The internet, World Wide Web, and 
telecommunication networks have complex structures, with a large 
number of heterogeneous nodes ( Barabási, 2013 ), most connected 
only to the central node, which participates in most connections, 
and thus has a strong inﬂuence in the network. Defense algo- 
rithms must consider the structural characteristics of complex net- 
works, using two types of strategies. Node immunization strategies 
immunize nodes with certain characteristics, and adaptive strate- 
gies change the network topology to reduce the maximum eigen- 
value of the network matrix, thereby changing the infectious dis-

work structure information, Xue and Hirche (2018) adjusted the

The above strategies were developed from a defense perspective 
and overlooked the proactive and dynamic features of attacks, and 
thus they adapt with diﬃculty to highly adversarial cybersecurity 
scenarios. In an attack-defense situation, the attacker will dynami- 
cally adjust the strategy according to the target, its defense behav- 
ior, and the situation. The interaction and dependence of attacking 
and defensive behaviors leads to the strong correlation and cou- 
pling of attack and defense strategies. However, the above defense 
strategies have poor adversarial analysis capabilities, and hence 
unpromising performance. 
Game theory can be used to study decision-making problems in 
a competitive, confrontational environment ( Maschler, 2020 ), and 
can improve the effectiveness of decision-making and enhance the 
applicability of selected strategies ( Miao et al., 2018 ; Liu et al., 
2021 ; Amaral et al., 2021 ; Farhadi et al., 2019 ; Sasahara and 
Sandberg, 2021 ). Pawlick et al. (2018) designed a signaling game 
model with a deception detection mechanism, obtained pooling 
and partially separating equilibria of the game, and designed a 
defensive deception strategy. Sengupta et al. (2018) studied the 
placement of intrusion detection systems (IDSs) in moving tar- 
get defense systems based on the Stackelberg game and obtained 
the optimal placement strategy by solving the game equilibrium. 
Kandoussi et al. (2020) established a stochastic game in cloud net- 
works and used attack graphs to simulate attacker-defender inter- 
action. A comprehensive defense strategy based on virtual machine 
migration and honeypots was proposed, which improved the sys- 
tem’s anti-attack ability. Wang and Song (2021) proposed a de- 
fense strategy against spam and phishing email under the condi- 
tion of limited resources. The defense strategy was obtained based 
on an evolutionary game, to effectively reduce the attack inten- 
sity. For cyberattacks on SCADA systems, Liu and Wang (2021) de- 
duced the time probability distribution of data and control system 
attack, and designed a network defense timing algorithm based 
on the FlipIt game to enhance the security of industrial control 
systems. 
A common problem of the above studies is the use of a dy- 
namic game model, whose obtained defense strategy is discrete 
and hysteretic. Hence the strategies cannot adjust in real time 
according to the current state, and the defense is passive. In 
reality, cyberattack and defense constitute a real-time, continu- 
ous process, and their strategies undergo high-frequency dynamic 
changes. Therefore, only continuous, real-time defense strategies 
are applicable to the actual attack-defense interaction to im- 
prove defense effectiveness. The differential game, as a theoretical 
method to describe the continuous control process in confronta- 
tion ( Friesz, 2010 ), meets the real-time requirements of defense 
strategies. Rao et al. (2020) proposed an isolation defense strategy 
based on a differential game against distributed denial of service 
(DDoS) attacks, and veriﬁed its effectiveness through numerical 
simulations. Gao et al. (2021) used a multi-attacker-defender in- 
teraction model based on differential games to study the relation- 
ship between attack-defense interactions and infection thresholds, 
and proposed an optimal decision algorithm for multi-defenders. 
Tan et al. (2021) integrated a differential game with a FlipIt game 
to study the decision-making in moving target defense, which 
improved the effectiveness of defense decision-making. Liu et al. 
(2021) studied the spread of security threats in wireless sensor 
networks based on a differential game, and proposed a method to 
suppress the growth of infected nodes, which reduced the defense 
cost. 
The above studies modeled the target as a uniform net- 
work model, assuming all nodes are homogeneous. But the in- 
ternet, the World Wide Web, and telecommunication networks 
have large scales and are complex, and their defense strate- 
gies are often not applicable to small-world, scale-free, and 
high-aggregation networks, and therefore are unsatisfactory. De- 
fense strategies for complex networks based on game the- 
ory are in the early stage of development, and only a few 
studies are available. Zhang and Xu (2019) studied the de- 
fense problem of heterogeneous device-to-device oﬄoading net- 
works, and modeled attack-defense interaction as a zero-sum 
differential security game. Ren and Zhang (2020) developed 
a game model to study the defense against attacks in Hon- 
eynet, and designed defense strategies for different network 
nodes. 
The decision-maker in the traditional game model is an in- 
dividual; therefore, although the above methods can obtain the 
optimal strategy for individual network nodes, they generally 
do not lead to global optimal network defense ( Dubey, 1982 ). 
The potential differential game combines the advantages of the 
potential ( Monderer and Shapley, 1996 ) and differential games, 
and has advantages in solving global optimal control problems 
( Fonseca-Morales and Hernández-Lerma, 2018 ). The classical dif- 
ferential game couples multiple optimal control problems, and 
has huge complexity due to the large number of nodes, and 
hence is infeasible. However, in the potential differential game, 
the attack and defense decision-maker have global network con- 
trol ability, and the problem is transformed to one of optimal 
control to obtain the overall attack and defense strategy, greatly 
reducing the computational complexity which gives it a huge 
advantage. 
In this study, according to the overall network defense objec- 
tive, we construct a differential game model for cyber defense in 
complex networks based on global optimal control. We conduct an 
adversarial analysis of the overall attack-defense strategies, and de- 
sign a defense decision algorithm based on the saddle point equi- 
librium of the game. Simulations on small-world and scale-free 
networks verify the effectiveness of the model and algorithm. The 
contributions of this study are as follows: 
(1) A cyberthreat propagation model is constructed to enable the 
microscopic evolutionary analysis of network node security 
states. A macroscopic attack and defense control strategy is pro- 
posed, and the game payoffs of the attacker and defender are 
calculated; 
(2) Based on potential differential game theory, an attack-defense 
potential differential game model with real-time analysis capa- 
bility is constructed, the calculation method of the saddle point 
equilibrium strategy is proposed, and a global optimal decision 
algorithm is designed based on the overall network defense ob- 
jective; 
(3) Veriﬁcation experiments are performed on typical complex net- 
works. A simulation environment with small-world and scale- 
free network characteristics is created, and the proposed model 
is compared with stochastic defense strategies, with results 
showing that it effectively increases the defense effectiveness 
of the networks.

2. Network attack-defense potential differential game 
2.1. Evolutionary analysis of network node states 
For a network with N nodes, the states of nodes will change 
in real time due to attack-defense interaction. We use the SIRS 
(Susceptible-Infected-Repaired-Susceptible) model to describe the 
evolution of the network nodes. There are three node states in the 
network: susceptible ( S ), infected ( I ), and repaired ( R ), and S i , I i , 
and R i represent the probability that node i is in those respective 
states. 
A susceptible node can provide services normally. Due to the 
lack of effective protection, it is easily attacked, at which time its 
state changes to infected. The attacker can use an infected node 
to attack other susceptible nodes and further spread the infection. 
The defender can repair the node so that it recovers from the in- 
fection. A repaired node has immunity and will not be reinfected 
for a short time. The immunity gradually disappears, and the node 
returns to the susceptible state. 
According to the above deﬁnition, based on the mean-ﬁeld 
model ( Pastor-Satorras and Vespignani, 2001 ), the differential 
equation for the transition of the network states is

3.3. Comparisons 
The defense decision algorithm based on a potential differential 
game in this study was compared with other algorithms, as shown 
in Table 1 . 
Adversarial analysis is to determine the angle of analysis of 
the attack and defense interaction and select the defense strategy. 
Rosenblatt et al. (2020) used ﬁxed attack and defense strategies, 
and did not consider strategy variations. Hu et al. (2018) adopted 
unilateral optimal control analysis, assuming that the attack strat- 
egy remained unchanged, and the defender selected strategies ac- 
cordingly. However, the attack-defense interaction is the core of 
network security. In an actual situation, attack and defense behav- 
iors are interdependent. Therefore, it is inappropriate to separate 
the attack and defense when studying a defense decision algo- 
rithm. The attack-defense game model was used by ( Pawlick et al., 
2018 ; Ren and Zhang, 2020 ), and this study to study the attack- 
defense interaction. The model selects a defense strategy based on 
the analysis of the impact of attack and defense strategies, and is 
suitable for actual network attack and defense scenarios. 
The decision-making basis is the method by which the defense 
strategy is obtained. Rosenblatt et al. (2020) used node connec- 
tivity as a reference to immunize nodes with certain character- 
istics. The strategy selection is simple, yet when the characteris- 
tics in the network are no longer obvious or the network struc- 
ture changes greatly, the effectiveness of the defense strategy may 
be greatly reduced. Hu et al. (2018) used the individual weight as 
a defense strategy, calculating the optimal weight by the optimal 
control method, and changing the network topology by adjusting 
the weight. On the basis of the payoff function, ( Pawlick et al., 
2018 ; Ren and Zhang, 2020 ), and the present study obtained the 
defense strategy by solving the game equilibrium. 
Strategy timeliness refers to the time during which the at- 
tack and defense strategies remain effective, which can repre- 
sent the timeliness of defense decision-making. Rosenblatt et al. 
(2020) used an immunization strategy, a ﬁxed defense strategy 
that was diﬃcult to adjust according to the attack-defense inter- 
action. Pawlick et al. (2018) adopted a stage game model, where 
the defender made decisions based on the game stage. It can- 
not respond in time when the network state changes, and hence 
the timeliness of the method is poor. In contrast, real-time de- 
fense decision-making was achieved by ( Hu et al., 2018 ; Ren and 
Zhang, 2020 ), and the present study, i.e., the algorithms have good 
timeliness. 
Comparing the results of selected defense strategies can 
achieve an individual-based or global optimum. Rosenblatt et al. 
(2020) used individual-based acquaintance immunization, which 
cannot achieve a global optimum. Pawlick et al. (2018) regarded 
the defender as the decision-maker, yet the defenders were still 
individuals, and the result was only an individual-based optimum. 
In Ren and Zhang (2020) , the individual defender calculated the 
defense strategy based on its own payoff, without considering the 
global payoff, which again can only achieve an individual-based 
optimum. In Hu et al. (2018) and this study, the defender selected 
the individual defense strategy from the perspective of the global 
payoff, which could maximize the overall defense payoff. 
The main shortcomings of the proposed method are: (1) The 
calculation cost of real-time attack and defense game analysis for 
large-scale complex networks is high, and defender need to have 
suﬃcient calculation ability and computing resources. (2) Network 
security state evolution analysis is the basis of attack-defense game 
modeling and strategy selection, which requires the high ability of 
network situation rapid awareness and dynamic update.

4.2. Defense effectiveness analysis 
To verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, 
Algorithm 1 was used to calculate the defense payoffs in the 
scale-free and small-world networks. Then 100 pairs of attack and 
defense strategies were randomly generated, and their payoffs 
calculated, which were compared with that of the method in this 
study. The results are shown in Fig. 3 . 
The x- axis is the number of the stochastic strategy, and the y- 
axis represents the defense payoff. The blue dots are the defense 
payoffs of the proposed method, which are 428.4 and 464.6. The 
three red lines represent the maximum, average, and minimum de- 
fense payoffs of the stochastic strategies, which were 212.3, 198.0, 
and 184.6, respectively, in the small-world network, and 233.5, 
215.2, and 198.4 in the scale-free network. It can be seen that in 
both networks, the defense payoffs of the proposed method in this 
study were signiﬁcantly higher than those of the stochastic strate- 
gies. 
4.3. Defense performance analysis 
(1) Comparison with typical defense strategies 
The defense payoffs of the proposed method were compared 
with those of the classical differential game, node immunization 
strategy ( Rosenblatt et al., 2020 ), and adaptive strategy ( Hu et al., 
2018 ). We carried out simulations on 20 randomly selected attack 
strategies. The results are shown in Figs. 4 and 5 . The x- axis is 
the serial number of the attack strategies, the y- axis is the de- 
fense payoff, and the horizontal lines represent the average payoff
of each strategy. 
Fig. 4 shows the defense payoffs of the above four algorithms in 
the small-world network. The average payoffs were 428.94, 322.60, 
282.85, and 394.68. The potential differential game in this study 
had the highest payoff, followed by the adaptive algorithm, differ- 
ential game, and immunization strategy. The defense effectiveness 
of the proposed method was 32.96%, 51.65%, and 8.68% higher than 
that of the other three methods. 
Fig. 5 shows the defense payoffs of the four algorithms in the 
scale-fee network. The average payoffs were 464.27, 299.87, 290.66, 
and 440.13. The proposed method had the highest payoff. The de- 
fense effectiveness of the proposed method was 54.82%, 59.73%, 
and 5.48% higher than that of the other three methods. 
Based on the results in the small-world and scale-free net- 
works, it can be seen that the defense payoffs of the proposed 
method and the adaptive strategy were signiﬁcantly higher than 
those of the other two methods, because they determine the op- 
timal defense strategies based on global payoffs. The immuniza- 
tion strategy had the highest stability of defense payoffs. When the 
immunization strategy is adopted, since the defender always im- 
plements the strategy for speciﬁc nodes, the attacker can only in- 
fect the remaining nodes. Hence the scope of infection is reduced, 
resulting in good stability. The adaptive method had the lowest 
payoff stability because the method is based on unilateral optimal 
control, and the defense strategy does not respond dynamically to 
the attack. When the attacker dynamically changes its strategy, it 
is diﬃcult for the defender to make timely adjustments, resulting 
in the largest ﬂuctuation of defense payoffs. The payoff stability of 
our method was similar to that of the classical differential game.

bution is relatively uniform in the small-world network, while the

there are large differences in the attack and defense strategies. 
5. Conclusion 
Most defense strategies in complex networks only focus on the 
inﬂuence of the defense action on network states, and overlook the 
impact of the attack action and attack-defense interaction. Defense 
strategies based on attack-defense adversarial analysis are mostly 
based on homogeneous mixing networks, which differ greatly from 
actual large-scale networks. In view of the above problems, com- 
plex networks and a potential differential game were integrated in 
this study, and the attack-defense interaction was analyzed from a 
global perspective to formulate the defense strategy. This method 
improved the effectiveness and applicability of defense decision. 
At the microscopic level, we constructed a cyberthreat propaga- 
tion model, established the differential equation for the transition 
of network states, and proposed a macro-level attack-defense po- 
tential differential game model. By calculating the saddle point 
equilibrium, the global optimal defense decision algorithm was ob- 
tained, which improved defense payoffs. Simulations were carried 
out in two classic complex networks, and the defense payoffs of 
different algorithms were compared, which veriﬁed the effective- 
ness of the proposed method. 
To implement the potential differential game model and de- 
fense decision algorithm in this study, a defender who masters the 
global information is needed, which may not exist in a real net- 
work. Thus, we will next introduce a penalty mechanism to the 
potential differential game and optimize the defense decision algo- 
rithm based on deep reinforcement learning, such that individual- 
based decision can also maximize the overall payoff of the net- 
work, thereby improving the applicability of the defense decision 
algorithm.
